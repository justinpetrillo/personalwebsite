Lifeworld, communicative action, and AI

Current independent work explores AI as a system for representing—and potentially reducing—the plurality of lived social reasoning. In the Honduras migration use case, branching interviews preserve how participants relate institutional conditions, belonging, and personhood. Structural and semantic analysis finds recurring interpretations without treating group membership as agreement.

  • Branching research interviews and participant meaning trees
  • Collective maps, structural comparison, and recurring interpretation groups
  • Participant-authored claims, scoped deliberation, and explicit human approval
  • Venture spaces for commitments, progress cycles, and renewed deliberation

Paper in development

System as AI: Legal Reflexivity and the Computational Reduction of the Lifeworld

The paper extends my New School work in communicative action and systems theory. It asks when language-model systems can mediate among institutions and lived experience without hiding the reductions made along the way, then translates that political problem into a specification that can be inspected and tested.

  • A bounded Reflexive Translation Automaton with explicit states, actor roles, invariants, and an audit ledger
  • Non-delegable transitions that preserve human authority over legal validity, collective meaning, and final action
  • Verification tests for provenance, fidelity, dissent retention, contestability, and authority discipline
